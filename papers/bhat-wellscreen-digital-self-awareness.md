# "In my defense, only three hours on Instagram": Designing Toward Digital Self-Awareness and Wellbeing

## Citation

**Authors:** Karthik S. Bhat, Jiayue Melissa Shi, Wenxuan Song, Dong Whi Yoo, Koustuv Saha  
**Year:** 2026  
**Conference:** CHI 2026

## Research Problem

What problem does this paper try to solve?

Most digital wellbeing tools focus on cutting screen time, but that framing assumes less time is always the goal. The authors ask a different question: what if the real lever isn't time, but whether people actually understand their own usage patterns at all?

## Main Idea

They built WellScreen, a lightweight tool that simply asks people to guess their smartphone usage before showing them the real number. The gap between the guess and the reality becomes the object of reflection, not the number itself.

## Method

How was the study conducted?

A two-week study with 25 college students. Each day,participants estimated their app usage before seeing the actual usage, then completed short wellbeing surveys.

## Key Findings

- People were consistently wrong in a specific direction: they underestimated productivity and social media use, but overestimated how much time they spent on entertainment apps.
- Just noticing that gap improved mood slightly, and most partcipants rated the tool as genuinely useful.
- In interviews, people said that exercise helped them recognize patterns and adjust their expectations, not necessarily use their phone less, but use it more on purpose.

## Open Questions I'm Sitting With

- The gap itself seems to be the interesting signal, not the raw usage number. That reframes what "useful fedback" might even mean.
- This was a two-week study. Does noticing the gap keep mattering after the novelty fades, or does it flatten out the way self-monitoring apps did in the Monge Roffarello paper?

## Why This Matters For My Research

What did I learn from this paper, how could it inspire future research?

This paper pushed me to seperate two things I'd been treating as one: awareness and restriction. WellScreen doesn't try to change behavior directly, it just closes a percpetion gap, and that alone did something. It made me think harder about what specifically should be reflected back to someone, not just that something should be. That question is close to the direction I"m developing for my own research.
